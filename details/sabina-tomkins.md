## Speaker: Sabina Tomkins
### Institution: University of Michigan
### Date: 14:00 GMT, 26th November, 2026

**Talk Title**: How should we actually use LLMs in human annotation workflows?

**Abstract**: Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used to annotate data in the computational social sciences and recent work has both critiqued and praised their ability to do so. A weak consensus has formed around the opinion that humans should be kept in the loop for these annotations, as a way to mitigate LLM errors and improve overall quality. Multiple frameworks have been proposed which leverage both human and LLM annotations to reduce cost and improve quality. However, each approach has concentrated on a narrow contribution, leaving holistic understanding untouched. This begs the question, how should we actually use LLMs with human annotators? Which workflow leads to the best gains and when? This talk will present the efforts of an interdisciplinary team to tackle this question.

**Bio**: Sabina Tomkins is an assistant professor at the University of Michigan School of Information and a faculty associate at the Center for Political Studies. Her work sits at the intersection of artificial intelligence and computational social science, developing context-aware methods to understand human behavior and social systems. Her research spans computational sustainability, political participation, and education policy, with a focus on using data-driven methods to support social impact. Tomkins earned her PhD from the University of California, Santa Cruz, and has held postdoctoral research appointments at Harvard University and Stanford University. Her work has received funding from the National Science Foundation and University of Michigan fellowships.
